# Professions and Crafting

[← Main proposal](../../README.md) · [Open World](../03-world-and-professions.md) · [Rewards](../11-rewards-and-acquisition.md)

## Purpose

Professions support lasting equipment alternatives, recipe specializations, cosmetic projects, utility items and a living player economy. New content should expand useful choices without issuing a stronger permanent equipment tier every update.

## Equipment and recipe boundaries

Crafted items remain inside the permanent power budget. Situational modifications replace or compete with existing effects rather than adding unbounded sockets, procs or bonuses. A recipe needs review through its output and interactions, not merely its rarity or item level.

Distinctive appearances can remain exclusive to an accomplishment. A combat effect that becomes necessary for another activity needs a reasonable functional alternative. An expensive auction listing alone does not prove accessibility.

## Sources of materials

| Source | Design role | Main constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heroic Dungeons | Accessible group materials and reviewed recipes | No mandatory circuit through every dungeon |
| Procedural Dungeons | Common materials as a reward option to test | Monitor supply, reward rates and easy-seed farming |
| World Bosses | Optional artifacts and specialized crafting choices | No monopolized raid-critical advantage |
| Regional activities | Local opportunities and optional projects | Necessary PvE supplies cannot depend exclusively on territorial victory |
| Reconstruction | Event supplies fund communal repairs | Avoid rare-material taxes; consume only the accepted delivery quantity |

For artifact-specific detail, see [World Boss crafting](world-bosses.md#world-boss-artifacts-and-crafting). For faucets and ownership, see [rewards and acquisition](../11-rewards-and-acquisition.md).

## Economy and long-term relevance

Demand should come from useful variety and optional projects. Evaluate material supply, speculation, low-population availability and profession dependencies before introducing rare bottlenecks. More recipes do not automatically create a healthy economy.

Progression must not reward producing and destroying trivial items solely to inflate a prestige counter. Any future activity-recognition rule needs validated objective identities and anti-repeat controls.
