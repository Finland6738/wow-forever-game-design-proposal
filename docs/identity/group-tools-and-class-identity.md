# Class Identity and Group Solutions

[← Classes](classes.md) · [Roles](class-roles-and-specializations.md) · [Heroics](../activities/heroic-dungeons.md)

## What the design question means

The question is whether a class's signature playstyle must also be an exclusive key required to finish an encounter. These are separate choices. An Artificer can uniquely tank through repairable dummies while other tanks solve the same encounter through their own defenses.

## Concrete examples

| Situation | Distinct tools and possible solutions | Encounter-design check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A dangerous trash caster heals allies | Interrupt its cast; control a different threat and focus it; use an intentionally supported line-of-sight pull | Confirm which alternatives really work in that room; do not promise every enemy is movable/control-vulnerable |
| An incoming damage event threatens the group | Tank positioning, prepared protection, healer planning or a support countermeasure | Give multiple valid plans rather than require one new class's unique button |
| A moving boss threatens the tank | Artificer repositions defenders and repairs; another tank uses its own movement and mitigation | Both preserve role responsibility; other tanks do not need copies of the dummy mechanic |
| A class has a recognizable signature | Swarm reserve, linked hexes, companion assignments or prediction timing | Keep the decision loop distinct even when several classes can interrupt or protect |

The selected direction is **multiple valid group solutions with distinct class tools**. This does not mean every class receives every ability or all combinations succeed without preparation. An encounter must not require a specific class; verify its actual alternative solutions before claiming composition-friendly access.

## What still needs specification

For each encounter list required functions, available alternatives, immune/unmovable targets and minimum coordination. For each class list signature mechanics, shared role tools and actual missing coverage. A generic label such as control or utility is insufficient evidence that a particular group can solve the encounter.
