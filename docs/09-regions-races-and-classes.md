# New regions, peoples and class identities

[← Main proposal](../README.md) · [Documentation index](README.md) · [Progression contract](01-progression-and-raids.md)

## Expand the world without importing a new power ladder

Future content updates can add regions, cultures, stories, dungeon environments and original playable identities while retaining level 60 and the permanent equipment budget. New areas should stand beside older content rather than functioning as the next mandatory level band.

“Season” describes a content-release cadence here. It does not imply character wipes, disappearing regions, expiring rank totals or a promise to deliver a new race and class every season. New playable identities have substantial art, animation, narrative, itemization and balance costs.

Keep the proposal's creative preference explicit: a grounded, sometimes ominous fantasy aesthetic, recognizable silhouettes, local cultures and adventures that belong in Forever's world. This is the project's desired direction, not an objective claim that other aesthetics are invalid or inconsistent with all Warcraft lore.

## Regions that add new kinds of play

Each proposed region should have a reason to exist beyond more daily quests. A region might combine a permanent story, an elite frontier, a settlement-defense cycle, expedition portal themes, profession projects, secrets or regional PvP where appropriate.

Use a selection of those systems rather than inserting every activity into every map. Connect geography, resources and enemy factions coherently. Preserve routes and useful rewards in older regions, and provide non-raid baseline preparation without requiring a tour of every new continent.

New regions also create maintenance commitments: pathing, quest continuity, event population, gathering supply, group formation and travel access. A growing map is valuable only if it remains usable and socially active.

## A race-selection policy

The direction favors **new playable peoples and distinctive concepts**, with their own silhouettes, cultures and reasons to participate in this world. Repeated cosmetic variations of an existing people are insufficient.

The following invented working names describe exploratory proposals. They are not established lore, announced races or a promise that a particular anatomy is technically feasible. Setting, roster, timeline and narrative fit must be reviewed before any selection.

| Exploratory candidate | Creative opportunity | Questions before a playable prototype |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gravemarch | Broad-built stone-country clans with a formidable communal identity | Scale, doors, armor fitting, animation and an independent cultural treatment |
| Brineveil | Serpentine coastal people with an aquatic identity and distinct movement | Land traversal, mounts, armor, collision, camera and a viable starting story |
| Stonewake | Towering mountain people with a presence shaped by regional mythology | Practical playable scale without losing identity; indoor encounters and equipment |
| Thornmaw | Jackal-like frontier people with a rough, recognizable silhouette | Language, social structure, faction relationships and depth beyond a hostile camp |
| Briarhide | Bristled thornland communities with distinctive materials and beliefs | Cultural agency, anatomy, animation and a coherent relationship with local regions |
| Veilbound | Shrouded magical people with a mysterious identity and unusual visual language | Origin story, introduction to the setting, armor expression and readable silhouettes |

These are candidates, not confirmed eligibility decisions or an approved release roster. No faction alignment, racial abilities or race/class combinations are committed. Each needs a culture, starting experience, social context and equipment coverage rather than only a model swap.

Racial mechanics must fit the same power policy. New options cannot be stronger permanent stat packages that make a race change the practical preparation step for a new raid. Avoid exclusive encounter-critical utility and rate their impact across PvE, PvP and Challenge profiles.

## A class-selection policy

A class needs a distinct combat decision loop, readable fantasy, coherent resource, intended group role and enough depth to sustain talents, gear choices and encounter design. A visual theme alone may belong in a specialization, cosmetic set or questline instead.

Every class uses a provisional invented name and must stand on its own mechanical identity. Stillguard, for example, researches guarded stances and deliberate counters; its defensive timing, resources and group role need to distinguish it from existing kits before it advances. A new name alone does not make a class a new design.

Similarly, avoid asserting that no existing class uses weapon magic, support, companions, curses or death-related abilities. Those are broad themes that can overlap. Evaluate the proposed loop against the roster instead of relying on claims that a fantasy has “never existed.”

## Class concept catalog

The following consolidates the brainstorming into **18 exploratory families**, each with an invented working name. Weapon-and-spell proposals form the single Aetheredge family. Role descriptions are prototypes, not a decision that all 18 become full classes.

| Concept | Distinctive loop to investigate | Main decision and risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Veilcantor | Build musical phrases, then perform an offensive, restorative or control sequence | When and where to perform; party amplification must not create a mandatory support slot |
| Cruciblewright | Prepare a limited mixture loadout; combine bombs, combat draughts and mutagen windows | Spend the correct preparation at the right moment; separate the class resource from profession shopping |
| Pactbinder | Direct a primary bonded entity, exchanging power between caster and companion | Commit the entity or recall it; pet pathing and loss recovery must be dependable |
| Oathseeker | Investigate a target, mark a tactical weakness and spend conviction on a judgment | Which threat to expose; distinguish the loop from existing holy or stealth combat themes |
| Doomseer | Accept a chosen curse that changes the cost and timing of predictions or protection | Which burden to manage; no unavoidable punishment for the healer or lore-dependent race lock |
| Hexweaver | Maintain linked hexes through a familiar and release them in a chosen order | Extend control or consume a curse; avoid becoming an existing caster with renamed effects |
| Cipherstalker | Gather encounter observations and convert deductions into tools or targeted openings | Which clue to exploit; combat analysis must stay readable and must not require secret enemy data |
| Aetheredge | Weave weapon actions into stored spell charges, then channel magic through a strike | Spend charges or preserve them for defense; prove differentiation from existing melee magic |
| Flintdancer | Manage ammunition, deliberate shots, tricks and reload windows | Shoot, reload or reposition; timing tolerance and range must suit an MMO |
| Saddlewarden | Coordinate mounted commitment and dismounted commands with a persistent companion | When to mount, dismount or direct the partner; indoor space and encounter collision are major risks |
| Ashcaller | Convert a bounded corpse-like encounter resource into controlled undead formations | Rebuild or expend the formation; use abstract resources where physical corpses are unavailable |
| Cogwarden | Deploy a small set of devices, reposition or repair them, then choose an overdrive window | Which creation receives resources; entity count, pathing and engineering-profession identity need limits |
| Gloamwarden | Establish a pursuit mark and use prepared positioning or control windows | Commit to pursuit or preserve an escape; avoid excessive mobility and unavoidable PvP control |
| Duskfletcher | Sequence marks, shadow shots and a finite spectral support effect | Consume a mark or maintain pressure; separate the loop from a cosmetic ranged-weapon variant |
| Sigilforger | Prepare a small rune arrangement, then trigger or reconfigure it during combat | Plan ahead or adapt; avoid a renamed existing rune-resource class |
| Wildmarshal | Coordinate a bounded set of persistent animal companions with complementary orders | Focus the pack or distribute tasks; prevent unit-count overload and companion-class duplication |
| Sanguivane | Build a blood resource and choose between reserve spending and bounded health costs | Risk health for timing without forcing healer subsidy; cap drains and recovery loops |
| Stillguard — requires prototype | Research guarded/offensive stances, resolve and deliberate counters | Prove a distinct resource and role; defensive timing must not merely duplicate an existing kit |

The first candidates worth prototyping are **Veilcantor, Cruciblewright and Cogwarden**, because their proposed loops can test different interactions: performance timing, preparation and controlled deployment. That is a design recommendation, not a priority guarantee; all three still carry significant overlap and group-balance risks.

## Thirteen mechanical studies from the broader brainstorming

These **original mechanical studies** explore specific decisions that might become a class, specialization, talent or encounter. Working names describe this proposal's concepts; numerical tuning and final identity remain open.

| Mechanical study | Adaptable direction | Constraint or reason it may remain a specialization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-form predator with combo spending | A Gloamstalker study with guarded and predatory forms | Player-controlled combat stance; real-world day/night cannot determine raid power; establish a distinct transformation loop |
| Music with an expendable swarm | A Blightchorister study combining phrases and bounded spectral vermin | Entity budget and readable control; investigate as a Veilcantor/Pactbinder branch rather than a compulsory separate class |
| Companion empowers the next ability | A Emberbond Sentinel study choosing the next offensive or defensive enhancement | Clear priming and consumption; the companion cannot add an unbudgeted second character |
| Apply and consume a cold state | A Rimecaller study of crowd shaping and delayed detonation | Strong specialization candidate; a shared frost theme does not by itself justify another class |
| Enchanted weapon charges and rare interventions | A Oathwish Duelist study with a finite encounter resource | No currency generation, unlimited resurrection or bypass of encounter failure rules |
| Mobile posture versus stationary remote casting | A Undertide Chanter study with a controlled focus and deliberate repositioning | Encounter accessibility, line of sight and safe disengagement; no indefinite invulnerable remote play |
| Deploy, repair and reposition automata | A Cogwarden branch centered on active device management | Bounded creations and support-role credit; no unrestricted swarm or collision griefing |
| Defensive stance versus risky offense | A Stillguard study of resolve, counters and deliberate openings | Tune output and incoming damage together; risky offense must not make the healer subsidize an uncontrolled bonus |
| Generated blood with optional health spending | A Sanguivane resource study | Bounded self-cost, no sacrifice into unavoidable death, no overhealing-to-infinite-resource conversion |
| Parries and partnered follow-ups | A paired-duelist study using timed response windows | Function independently; a specific second player or class cannot be mandatory |
| Ammunition and a deliberate precision shot | A Flintdancer branch emphasizing reload and release timing | Server-authoritative timing with latency tolerance; no frame-perfect requirement for ordinary play |
| School selection followed by a rune-enhanced spell | An Runewake Scholar or Sigilforger study | Manageable input complexity and class identity; investigate a specialization before adding a whole class |
| Mounted/dismounted play with shared companion damage | A Saddlewarden/Briarstrider study | Bounded damage transfer, clear companion recovery, pathing and viable indoor play |

These studies are a record of alternatives, not 13 additional approved classes. They may merge, become talents, remain NPC encounter mechanics or be rejected after testing. The class catalog and the studies must not be added together as a release count.

## Group roles, support and horizontal balance

The first class prototypes should fit established tank, healer or damage slots while trading part of their own output for a bounded support contribution. A pure fourth support role would require a separate encounter and group-formation experiment; do not assume five-player content can absorb it without redesign.

Cap and scope amplification carefully across 5–20 players. Multiple Veilcantors, devices or marks must not multiply party damage indefinitely. A group without a new class needs a valid strategy, and no new raid should require a newly purchased, leveled or unlocked identity.

Test class kits against permanent baseline profiles, Normal/Veteran contextual effects, Challenge templates, procedural scaling, invasion participation and PvP control. New classes cannot arrive with higher-budget gear or an exception to normalization. Combat pets, swarms and machines also need entity budgets and performance tests in crowded events.

## Development gates

1. Establish setting fit, player fantasy and a loop that differs from the existing roster.
2. Prototype one representative combat loop and role before promising several specializations.
3. Test readability, input complexity, accessibility, latency and group composition.
4. Evaluate animation, equipment coverage, pathing, world traversal and technical cost.
5. Validate the permanent power budget and activity-specific normalization.
6. Expand only if the concept offers durable play value rather than a novelty model or stronger racial/class package.

Playable unlocks, if any, must not depend on high prestige rank, prior raid completion or winning territorial PvP. Preserve accessible leveling and baseline preparation. Existing characters and classes should remain complete choices as the world expands.
