# Social design, simple rules and earned prestige

[← Main proposal](../README.md) · [Documentation index](README.md)

## The experience worth preserving

This proposal starts from a social memory: people often remember who helped them, the group that recovered from a difficult evening and the friendships that formed along the way. Equipment and encounters create reasons to meet; lasting relationships give those experiences meaning.

Forever should grow while preserving that foundation. A permanent level cap supports continuity, but continuity also needs recognizable places, reliable companions, meaningful class contributions and accomplishments other players can understand.

## Put cooperation at the center of the endgame

The main endgame achievements should come from group adventures: tactical dungeons, raids, expeditions, settlement defense, regional objectives and community projects. Grouping should create opportunities that simply repeating an efficient solo routine does not reproduce.

| Design goal | Practical behavior | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need other people for meaningful adventures | Encounters reward coordination, preparation and complementary responsibilities | A solo checklist supplies the real mandatory power while group content is optional decoration |
| Recognize several contributions | Tanking, healing, control, preparation and objectives matter alongside damage | One damage number becomes the only public measure of value |
| Help groups stay together | Clear objectives, recovery checkpoints, reconnect support and straightforward roster management | One disconnect or failed attempt destroys the evening's opportunity |
| Build local community identity | Settlement projects, familiar meeting places, class identity and persistent collections | Every update replaces all meaningful locations and relationships |
| Reward teaching and reliability | Useful group information and voluntary mentoring support | A compulsory mentoring currency creates another farm or exploitable transaction |

Solo leveling, travel, profession preparation and exploration remain useful parts of a persistent world. They should help players enter its social life, rather than replace the cooperative endgame or become a mandatory daily preparation tax.

## Understandable rules with depth in execution

The game should explain what a player can do, why an effect works and what an encounter requires. Mechanical depth can come from timing an interrupt, protecting another player, selecting a target or coordinating movement. It does not require dozens of simultaneous hidden conditions.

Use readable warnings, consistent ability language and clear failure feedback. A small set of mechanics can interact in interesting ways, provided the group has room to learn and recover. Avoid mandatory external tools as a substitute for understandable encounters.

Simple rules do not require easy outcomes. A clearly telegraphed mechanic can still demand preparation, practice and dependable teamwork. Conversely, opaque mechanics are not automatically meaningful difficulty.

## Accessible entry and earned completion

An accessible equipment baseline removes an inherited gearing barrier. It does not grant a boss kill, guarantee full completion or entitle every character to every prestigious reward.

Normal provides a manageable commitment for reasonably prepared groups. Veteran asks for stronger execution and organization. Both have their own contextual equipment progression; neither supplies the other's required gear currency. Permanent repertoire abilities are different: each difficulty teaches only its own, so collecting both intentionally involves both completions.

| Reward type | What earns it | Why the boundary matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry-ready permanent equipment | A finite, accessible preparation path through several activities | A returning player can participate without replaying years of raid updates |
| Local raid progression | Valid participation and rewards inside that update/difficulty | Power supports that adventure without becoming a future entry gate |
| Veteran prestige | Completing the stated Veteran objectives | Achievement remains recognizable without exporting permanent power |
| Challenge medals and records | Validated execution under the relevant ruleset | Practice and mastery remain the activity's purpose |
| Civic recognition | Meaningful defense or recovery work | Supporting the world counts without awarding combat advantage |
| Participation ranks | Cumulative, eligible activity under published scoring rules | Long-term contribution is visible without being mislabeled as skill |

Prestigious cosmetics may require difficult accomplishments. Keeping them available for future groups does not mean granting them automatically. Avoid removing rewards solely to manufacture urgency; players can earn recognition by overcoming the content while it remains supported.

## Respect commitment without inventing obligations

Players may choose to devote many evenings to one activity. The game should support that commitment without requiring the same schedule from everyone else. Independent progression paths, permanent collections and bounded preparation help reconcile those preferences.

Avoid universal weekly currencies, compulsory solo errands before raids and cross-difficulty double farming. Equally, do not make a prestige badge a hidden access requirement or a new class an obligatory group slot.

The double-farming concern applies to equipment preparation. Returning for an exclusive historical ability, or completing both difficulties to collect both options, is now a deliberate progression goal. That choice accepts some optimization pressure; group availability and acquisition effort need evaluation rather than a blanket claim that every path is interchangeable.

Social friction requires judgment. Forming a group can be part of the experience; unexplained travel failures, opaque prerequisites and resource-losing retries add little social value. Preserve meaningful dependence on people while reducing preventable administrative barriers.

## Questions for every new feature

1. What cooperative decision does this create?
2. Can different roles make a visible contribution?
3. Are the rules understandable before repeated failure?
4. Does the reward communicate an accomplishment that was actually earned?
5. Does preparation remain bounded and independent of unrelated activities?
6. Does the feature strengthen a lasting place, group or relationship?

These principles guide design review. They do not replace playtests, player disagreement or the need to measure whether the resulting social experience is enjoyable.
